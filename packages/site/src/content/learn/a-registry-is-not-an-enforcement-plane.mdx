  The argument, as agreed:

  1. What a registry answers.
     Discovery and distribution — which servers exist, where they are, what
     they claim to expose. Genuinely useful, and the ecosystem is right to
     build it. Be generous here; the piece is not against the registry.

  2. What it does not answer.
     Whether *this* caller may make *this* call *now*. A registry is a
     catalogue: it describes, it does not decide. Nothing in the protocol
     turns an entry into a refusal.

  3. Why the gap widens rather than closes.
     Every extension — tasks, interceptors, the registry itself — adds call
     shapes. Each one is another thing that can happen and nothing new that
     can say no. Growth of the surface is growth of the gap.

  4. The distinction to leave the reader with.
     Catalogue vs verdict. An enforcement plane is not a better registry; it
     is the other half — the part that answers with allow or deny, on the
     path, before the call reaches anything.
